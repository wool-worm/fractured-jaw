In all ways we have compelled the world, and are thus compelled by the world, into an addiction to immediacy. Performances in film no longer offer escape, in the sense that they are composed and conducted inside their own world, one which we have to consciously enter to experience. They are instead projected through the screen and at the viewer, all affectations are spelled out in exhaustive detail so that there is no room for misinterpretation. Music is endlessly compressed so that the listener should never be forced to peer into the recording to find what is hidden, lest they be surprised by exuberant dynamics. The recording is flattened, not only to avoid the ill effects of surprise and mystery, but also to improve the playback on portable speakers. Our addiction is not only to experiential immediacy but also to a technological one. The technology that itself provides the affective immediacy, precludes a lingering or intentional experience of the thing. It demands that we be on the move, going somewhere or doing something else that is more important that what is truly at hand. In this addiction we have become increasingly self-obsessed and thus afflicted with a certain totalitarian mindstate, one in which all external experience must be a servitor to our own need for clearness and surety that nothing surprising will ever happen.

In all realms we are increasingly compelled towards an addiction to immediacy. This addiction requires nothing less than our complete and infinitely divided attention, and demands nothing short of absolute smoothness and transparency in its search for maximum efficiency. In immediacy, there can be no lingering, no peering into, no surprises. In this condition, we must abolish all sense of the alien. In this addiction we have become increasingly self-obsessed and afflicted with a certain totalitarian mindstate, one in which all external experience must be a servitor to our own need for clearness and surety that nothing unexpected will ever happen. Synchronicity has evaporated into coincidence, coincidence has evaporated into a vestigial recognition that something has perturbed the otherwise smooth arrival of some future state. It is critical to understand that the craving is for a false immediacy, in the same way that an addict reaches for a false oblivion.
