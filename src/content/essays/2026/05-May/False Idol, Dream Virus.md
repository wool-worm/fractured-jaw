### Technofascist Steamrollers

In all realms we are increasingly compelled towards an addiction to immediacy. In film, music, public and private performance, and in the optimization culture: homes so smart, they maintain themselves; vehicles so automated, they no longer need a driver; expression so intuitive, one no longer needs to think. This addiction requires nothing less than our complete and infinitely divided attention, and demands nothing short of absolute smoothness and transparency in its search for maximum efficiency. Always seeking a more direct interface with our psyche, the capitalist dream virus infiltrates and attaches itself to any last resistances, areas in which the effort exerted toward a thing is greater than the monetary value it receives from it. The advantages of roughness and difficulty are not detectable by it and so they are merely obstacles to the inevitable procession towards decreased resistance, increased ease, and a—falsely perceived—perfect interface with reality.

Despite the belief that ease is paramount to contentment, it is in an environment of resistance where humanity thrives. Roughness of experience provides an index for how we experience the world, much like the music on a vinyl record is indexed to the stylus by the grooves that underlay it. An environment of ease provides no such index. It is an experience diffuse, unmoored, and disinherited of all relation to the exterior. Without a connection outside itself, the object of attention turns inward, as that is the sole reference point available. Sustaining this condition for long enough then feeds a growing expectation for the same nutrients that created it, a cycle that is mirrored at the interpersonal, community, and societal levels.

Popular film and music become an experience of receiving rather than participating; one is no longer required to give part of themselves away in the process. Homes have become cells where, with the right technology, it is possible to both never truly attend to the state of the space and to perfectly surveil the outside world. Never before has there been a time where it was widely possible to have every physical need met without ever leaving the home. Delivery apps naturally support this state of affairs, providing seamless access to all material needs while never requiring interaction with another, who merely serves an instrumental, transient, and subservient role. The speed of automated life promises to liberate us from the obligations of living itself, though the purported free time we gain in the transaction always slips through our fingers. The perfection of ease is tantamount to the rejection of our dialogue with life.

### Living Ghosts of The Spectacle

In the realm of media, our dialogue takes the form of giving oneself away to the images, sounds, and affect of the performance. The post-dialogue world inverts this expectation, compelling the performance to give itself away to the audience. This mode favors a flattened, effusive, spewing-out style of communication, such that nuance is pre-masticated and fully clarified. While the independent undercurrent of film and music has never been stronger, the state of popular media, art engineered for mass consumption, has languished. Immense budgetary and boardroom pressure from major studios and publishers has hampered creativity as the industry becomes increasingly risk averse.

The language of popular cinema is dull and plain. Metaphors are fully explained, symbols referenced to the point of exhaustion, messages are partially digested into a nutritious paste. You can’t choke on it but you can’t enjoy it either. The bioreactor of the focus group ensures that everything is clearly delivered and that all nuance is eradicated. In doing so, any thinking is rendered unnecessary and all interpretations converge to a single point. In such an environment, there is no haze through which communication can be properly dispersed into a spectrum of understandings–rather there are only a multitude of immediate apprehensions circumambulating that one point of absolute certainty. As communication and interpretation converge, all potential perspectives are assimilated into a unitary that operates with pure authority over its subjects.

This is not to say that there were never films meant for pure entertainment in the past, nor is it to say that there is no place for them in the present. There is always a need for escape, something to engage with to disengage from everything else. And this is also not a highbrow argument to sow an artificial division with “low” entertainment. It is congruent to argue both that there is a place for pure entertainment and that entertainment which immediately discloses itself is not a true escape. In immediacy there can be no peering into, no surprises. Because it is fully disrobed for the audience, this type of film is experienced entirely in the ego. When the secrets of a film are withheld, the audience is forced to participate to unearth them; they are made to step out into an intermediary space. The true escape of art does not come from it being spoken aloud, but from stepping out of the inner world and into the weird dimension that is unspoken.

Dynamics, rubato, and silence give meaning to what is said rather than the inverse. The two are interleaved with each other. Music can instigate peering into in much the same way as film. The pauses that last almost, but not quite, too long on Miles Davis’s *Kind of Blue*. The imperceptible building and sudden diminishing synth pads on Aphex Twin’s *Selected Ambient Works*. Plumbing the fractal depths of Giant Claw’s *Mirror Guide*. The intimate and vulnerable feelings not-quite-said on SOPHIE’s exuberant hyperpop or Oklou’s *Choke Enough*. Every example mandates attending to the spaces between. One’s participation is not just requested, it is required, and it is rewarded. That these albums exist and are discoverable is testament to this golden age of independent art. Sadly, most music that dominates the charts stops short of these qualities. Publishers who are afraid to leave anything hidden or undisclosed betray their listeners by pouring out and scattering meaning. The production compresses out of existence any opportunity to lean in and to be blown back by an ecstatic outburst. The haze of mystery having been wiped away, the chance for independent meaning-making nullified, we are delivered into the gentle totalitarianism of transparency.

### Chinese Takeout and Romance

To maintain this mindstate, one must abolish all sense of the alien, as this alone poses the greatest threat to the self-primacy that is promoted by it. Therefore all interactions with others must be strictly limited to terms of transaction and measurement. To keep the individual atomized and segregated is to prevent any chance of exposure that may erode the grip of the system that leverages them. The ultimate end state of this effort is to mediate all interactions through technology. In this way, the impression of clearness can be ensured and any roughness or complications can be smoothed away. Through technology there is no longer a risk of a negative experience, but there is also no chance of a positive one. The neutral space between those two poles is simpler to digest, clear to both the orchestrator and the participant. Technologies of interaction tend to dissolve bonds of trust by claiming for their own the trust that would otherwise be built between the self and the other.

Ceding this utterly human function to algorithms has only accelerated the dissolution of trust across society. Intuition and communication are substituted with ratings, escrow, and verification. There is no doubt that these KPIs of human connection offer a concrete and cheap alternative to the status quo. Since all interaction can be reduced to five out of five stars and a checkmark, we have surely reached the terminus of social development. Rather, the mere existence of these systems prime us with fantasies of danger that rarely materialize. In the popular narrative, it is incomprehensible to even consider a world without the nonstop surveillance of Uber and DoorDash drivers. How would we know if our service workers were behaving if we couldn’t track their movements? How would we be able to preemptively reject our Uber ride if we can’t see that their rating is below 4.7 stars? The systems of immediacy and clarity sell both the cause and the cure.

Our newfound technologies promise us cheap products, transport, food, and most perniciously, love. If anything has been constant in human experience, surely it has been the difficulty of finding and sustaining love. So it is not surprising that this sphere was one of the first to be invaded by 21st-century capitalist mediation. What better seduction to offer than love delivered to one’s doorstep, as automated and simple as ordering takeout. Apps, frictionless and perfectly tuned to enrapture their users, have trapped society’s collective romantic impulse within an infinite scroll of profiles. When you have five photos and ten prompts to fully describe yourself, who could ever be vulnerable?

The result: what was promised as a frictionless experience is indeed so, but so lacking in anything resembling reality as to nearly be a game. Insofar as these technologies have their own currency, microtransactions, and metastrategies, it is hard to distinguish them from games at all. And yet, we have been unable to extract ourselves from this arrangement. The collective fear of the other and the demand for certainty about them has successfully incepted the technology that anesthetizes that fear. With no obvious alternatives, the individual invests themselves further into this system, believing that it is the only avenue of discernment. Much in the same way that delivery apps have adopted mandatory verification and ratings, so too are dating apps trending in this direction. Our revulsion toward mystery paired with the addiction to immediacy rejects the eroticism of wild experience.

Of course these systems still do not go far enough. The frenzy to verify human beings using AI-powered ID scanning and facial recognition has extended beyond any legitimate applications and into the absurd. As of the writing of this article, there is pending rulemaking that will require active driver monitoring to start and operate a car; your car will not need you and it won’t trust you either. No doubt this constant surveillance, much like the imposition of the TSA in the wake of 9/11, offers an effective palliative treatment for obsession with clarity and data. It does come at a cost: inescapable psychological terrorism that extends from public to private life.

### The Prisoner and The Besieged

There has never been a time in human history where a person could have all their needs met without leaving their home. Using our technologies of immediacy, of food, of products, of love, we have been empowered to complete our lifecycles in the cocoon of the smarthome. Like a medieval city under siege, the smarthome is designed for operating with minimal inputs while offering maximum security. It is the truest abode of immediacy. Here we can sleep, wake up, work a remote job, and control all functions of the home with digital assistants. When the trash bags are low, they are automatically delivered. When you run out of food, the fridge will order more. Lights are voice and motion activated, smart speakers follow you with entertainment in tow. Smart coffee pots prepare your morning drink before you wake up. There are even smart toasters that will prevent you from overexerting yourself while making breakfast. With every necessity immediately provided as an extension of our will, we can fully retreat to the interior life. A properly actualized smarthome untethers us from all external obligations and promotes a commensurate neglect of our space.

But it seems even in this neglect, there still remains some curiosity about the outside world, insofar as it pertains to protection and safety. As smart appliances are the organs of the home, the surveillance system is its shield and herald. Making no attempt at concealment, the smart cameras, smart doorbells, and smart floodlights pock the outside of the home, definitive statements to all who are outside reminding them which side of the door they are on. From their smartphone, from anywhere in the world, the owner can indulge in surveillance at will. Doubtless, systems like these have helped to solve and prevent crime. Cases have made local news, been popularized by technology companies, and passed around neighborhood Facebook groups. Regardless, in the vast majority of cases, their primary function is to assuage the anxieties arising from a lack of immediacy about all that is *outside*.

There is common knowledge and criticism of “hostile design,” park benches adorned with spikes to prevent resting, infrasonic emitters to prevent loitering. What there has not been enough discussion of is a new class of this design ethos, “parasitic architecture.” This proposed style, which implicates the smarthome and its accessories, is not so overt as spiked benches. It primarily acts against the occupant rather than the outsider by inducing a dependence that perpetuates its own existence. Its existence is also predicated on a central anxiety that things will not be clear, flat and immediate. Any indication of roughness is simply another chance to optimize, another product waiting to increase ease and and forestall feelings that “not enough has been done yet.”

### A False Idol Melted

Immediacy demands of us that we must never part with the self in pursuit of understanding. One must never leave themselves behind while stepping into a wild dimension. It requires that all messages and symbols be flattened into a unitary comprehension. Immediacy, in its inability to integrate meaning from the other, pronounces: “ego is god and other is servant.” In the grips of addiction, our worsened self-obsessiveness has left us afflicted with a totalitarian mindstate, one in which all external experience must be a servitor to our own need for clearness and surety that nothing surprising will ever happen. Synchronicity has evaporated into coincidence, coincidence has evaporated into a vestigial recognition that something has perturbed the otherwise smooth arrival of some future state.

What hope are we left with then? How can we deliver ourselves from this condition? There is an undeniable draw of connection, clear-seeing, and direct experience. Are these things that will remain ever out of our grasp? That despite our best efforts, they are not things we can construct, not gifts we can receive? It is paradoxical that, in a wicked turn of irony, seeking clarity and immediacy awakens in them their own antitheses and mutual annihilation. Our apparent perdition vanishes when we realize two facts. What we and our culture lionize as immediacy is nothing of the sort. It is simply the single most mediated condition imaginable. Apps, screens, algorithms, the camera in the car, the five photo dating profile, GPS tracking your delivery driver. It stretches the imagination to concoct a more artificial experience, and yet it is widely seen as transparency. The irony instead lies in that the true immediacy beyond the false, the unmediated contact with another, is precisely what this system prevents. That somehow we, in this purely receptive role, believe we can interface with something that is fundamentally participatory, unlocks the unreality. We see the golden calf for what it is.

Our second fact is that the true immediacy and direct wild experience we seek is never something we can receive. No amount of paid retreats, ayahuasca ceremonies, or authenticity gurus will help because the cure is not for sale. The balance between giving and taking in our dialogue with life is precisely the fulcrum upon which the constitution of our self’s primacy rests. The self is exposed only once it has been left behind, and is only dissolved once it is exposed. Immediacy is not a thing you get, it is the thing you let go of. With each of these facts in hand, we can lift the golden calf from its altar and cast it onto the flames, together reveling in the strange wheeling sparks lofted towards the nascent alien sky.
